# Marketing and Branding Hooks

**Hook 2001**
Hook: Marketing isn’t about being the best—it’s about being remembered.
Example: Nobody says Pepsi is better, but everyone remembers Coke.
Psychology: Memory bias → familiar = trusted.
**Hook 2002**
Hook: If your brand doesn’t trigger emotion, it won’t trigger sales.
Example: Nike makes you feel unstoppable, not just “sold sneakers.”
Psychology: Emotional resonance → feelings drive decisions.
**Hook 2003**
Hook: Stop chasing trends. Start building trust.
Example: Apple doesn’t need TikTok dances. It has loyalty.
Psychology: Trust \> novelty effect.
**Hook 2004**
Hook: Nobody cares about your product. They care about their problem.
Example: Headspace doesn’t sell meditation—it sells stress relief.
Psychology: Problem-solving bias.
**Hook 2005**
Hook: The best ads don’t look like ads.
Example: Old Spice turned commercials into memes.
Psychology: Entertainment bias → we share what feels fun.
**Hook 2006**
Hook: If your brand voice sounds like everyone else—you’re invisible.
Example: Wendy’s Twitter roasts gave them a whole new identity.
Psychology: Differentiation effect.
**Hook 2007**
Hook: You don’t need more followers—you need more buyers.
Example: A 1,000-follower account can outsell a 50K one with the right offer.
Psychology: Vanity metric correction.
**Hook 2008**
Hook: The best logos don’t sell—they remind.
Example: McDonald’s golden arches = instant craving.
Psychology: Visual cue bias.
**Hook 2009**
Hook: Branding isn’t what you say—it’s what they say about you.
Example: Tesla doesn’t run ads—its customers do.
Psychology: Word-of-mouth persuasion.
**Hook 2010**
Hook: People don’t share products—they share stories.
Example: Dove’s “Real Beauty” campaign went viral as a story, not a soap ad.
Psychology: Narrative spread bias.

**Hook 2051**

**Hook 2052**

**Hook 2054**

**Hook 2055**

**Hook 2056**

**Hook 2057**

**Hook 2058**

**Hook 2059**

**Hook 2060**
